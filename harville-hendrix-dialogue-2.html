SECTION 5 OF 5Application & debrief

Part 1: Apply the ideas – role-play

Choose one situation. Prepare what you would say, using the principles of dialogical conversation: listen without criticism, check your understanding, recognise the other person's point of view and show empathy.

Choose a situation above to begin.

Part 2: Practise a dialogical conversation

Work with a partner. Choose a topic on which you have different opinions. Take turns to speak and listen. Do not interrupt or prepare a rebuttal while the other person is speaking.

Mirroring

Show that you heard

“So, what you're saying is...”

“Let me check that I understood you...”

Validation

Recognise their viewpoint

“I can see why you might think that.”

“That makes sense from your perspective.”

Empathy

Recognise their feelings

“I can understand that you feel...”

“That must have been difficult for you.”

Part 3: Debriefing questions

  1. Why do you think it can be difficult to listen without criticising or defending yourself?
  2. Which of the three stages – mirroring, validation or empathy – would be most difficult for you? Why?
  3. How could dialogical conversation improve communication in your workplace or team?
  4. Hendrix says that listening is the first act of love. Do you agree? Can the same idea apply to professional relationships?
